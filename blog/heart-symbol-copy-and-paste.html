Heart Symbol Copy and Paste: Text Hearts, Emoji Hearts and What Makes Them Different

Updated November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Use the tap-to-copy picker above to grab a heart quickly, or copy one from the lists below and paste it into your bio, caption or chat. Some hearts are plain text characters (♥ ♡ ❤) and some are colored emoji (💜 🧡 💖), and they don't always look the same on every phone. This guide sorts them, explains why they differ, and shows how to pick one that will display reliably.

Heart symbols to copy and paste

Classic text hearts: ♥ ♡ ❤ ❣ ❥

These are Unicode text characters. They usually take on the color and size of the text around them, which makes them a good fit for minimal, matching styles.

♥ is the solid heart (the card-suit heart) and ♡ is the outline version. ❤ is a heavier, rounder heart. ❣ is a heart exclamation mark, and ❥ is a heart with a slanted, stylized shape.

Emoji hearts by color: ❤️ 🧡 💛 💚 💙 💜 🖤 🤍 🤎

Emoji hearts are drawn in color by your device's emoji font, so the exact artwork depends on the phone or app.

Expressive emoji hearts: 💕 💖 💗 💓 💞 💘 💝 💔

Two hearts, sparkles, arrows and ribbons make these feel more playful than a single plain heart. 💔 is the broken heart.

How to copy a heart

  1. Tap the heart you want in a tap-to-copy list. If tapping does not copy it on your browser, use the select-and-copy method below instead.
  2. Open the app where you want to use it.
  3. Tap and hold in the text field, then choose Paste.

If you're copying from ordinary text on a page instead, tap and hold to select the heart, drag the handles so only the heart is highlighted, then choose Copy. Selecting extra spaces will paste them too.

Heart symbol vs heart emoji: what is the difference?

Heart characters are Unicode text, not images

A heart symbol like ♥ is a character in Unicode, the standard that lets different devices agree on what each character is. It is stored as text, like a letter, not as a picture. Your device chooses a font to draw it, which is why you can paste it almost anywhere text is accepted.

Text presentation vs emoji presentation

Some characters can be drawn two ways: as a plain monochrome glyph (text presentation) or as a colorful emoji picture (emoji presentation). An invisible helper character called a variation selector (U+FE0F) asks for the emoji version. The red ❤️ is the heart character ❤ (U+2764) followed by that selector. Without it, you get ❤, which a device may draw as a plain glyph.

Why ❤ and ❤️ can look different

They are not quite the same sequence. ❤ may appear as a flat, black or text-colored heart, while ❤️ asks for the colored emoji. Different apps handle this differently, so the same pasted heart can turn red in one place and stay plain in another.

Why the same heart looks different on different devices

Unicode defines which character a heart is, not how it is drawn. Every operating system and many apps use their own fonts and emoji artwork, so the shape, shading and color vary. That is normal and not a sign that you copied the wrong character.

Decorative and heart-like characters

Ornament hearts such as ❦ and ❧

These come from sets of typographic ornaments, not the regular heart group. ❦ (U+2766) is a floral heart and ❧ (U+2767) is a rotated floral heart bullet. They suit dividers and decorative touches.

Characters that only look like hearts, such as ღ

ღ is a letter from the Georgian alphabet, but it is often used online as a heart-shaped decoration. It can be fun, but it is a letter being used decoratively.

Why look-alikes are less reliable than real heart characters

Real heart characters are widely supported. Borrowed letters and ornaments depend on whether the font in use contains them. If the font lacks them, the device substitutes another font or shows an empty box. Use look-alikes as optional decoration, not as something your message depends on.

Which heart should you use where?

Bios and usernames

Plain text hearts like ♡ and ♥ are the safest bet, since they follow your text color and need no emoji font. Usernames are often restricted to letters, numbers and a few symbols, so a heart may be rejected or stripped. Test it before you commit, and keep your name readable without it.

Captions and comments

Emoji hearts are easy to recognize in captions and comments and add color. A single 💖 or 🤍 at the end of a sentence is usually enough.

Chat messages and notes

In chats, emoji hearts are normally fine because most messaging apps are built for emoji. In personal notes, documents and plain text files, ♡ or ♥ may be more predictable because they don't depend on emoji artwork.

Matching heart color to mood without overdoing it

Colors carry loose, culture-dependent associations, and nothing in Unicode fixes their meaning. The same color can mean different things to different people, so treat these as common habits, not rules:

  • ❤️ is the default for love or warmth.
  • 💙 💚 often read as calm or friendly.
  • 🖤 🤍 are popular for a minimal or aesthetic look.
  • 💔 signals sadness or heartbreak, so use it with care.

Why a heart shows up as a box or looks wrong

Missing glyphs and font fallback

When the current font lacks a character, the device looks for another font that has it. If none does, you see an empty rectangle or a question-mark box. This is more common with decorative characters than with ♥ or ♡.

Newer emoji on older software

Emoji are added to Unicode over time. A newer heart emoji may appear as a box or a pair of unrelated symbols on a device whose software predates it. Classic hearts like ♥, ♡ and ❤ are older and tend to be more widely supported than newly added colors.

Apps and fields that strip or restrict characters

Some forms, username fields and older systems accept only a limited set of characters. They may remove your heart, replace it, or reject the entry.

Quick fixes

  • Switch to a classic ♥ or ♡.
  • Replace a very new emoji with an older, more common heart.
  • Copy or type ♥ or ♡ on its own, not as part of a longer pasted string. Pasted when copied alone, these are single characters with no U+FE0F selector attached, so they avoid the text-versus-emoji switch that can turn ❤ into ❤️ or the reverse. Some apps may still draw ♥ as an emoji.
  • Send a test message or preview the post before publishing.

Even with these steps, you can't control what fonts another person's device has.

Using hearts accessibly

How screen readers may handle symbols

Screen readers may announce a heart by its name, shorten it, or skip it, and behavior can vary between devices and apps. Since you can't predict what a listener will hear, don't let a heart carry essential meaning.

Don't replace words with hearts

"I ♥ this recipe" may be read in an unexpected way, or not make sense when read aloud. Writing "I love this recipe ♥" keeps the message clear even if the heart is skipped or garbled.

Keep repeated hearts to a minimum

A long string like ♥♥♥♥♥♥ can be read aloud repeatedly, and it also clutters the text for everyone else. One or two hearts is usually enough. Put them at the end of a sentence, not in the middle of words.

How to type hearts on Android without copy and paste

Use your keyboard's emoji panel

Most Android keyboards have an emoji key, often a smiley face, near the space bar. Open it, look for the hearts, and tap one to insert it directly. This works for emoji hearts in any text field that accepts them. The exact layout depends on your keyboard.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a heart symbol on my keyboard?

On Android, open your keyboard's emoji panel and choose a heart such as ❤️ or 💜. For a plain text heart like ♥ or ♡, copy it from a list and paste it. The result depends on your keyboard and the app you are typing in.

What is the plain heart symbol text character?

The solid text heart is ♥ (the black heart suit), and its outline version is ♡ (the white heart suit). Both are regular Unicode characters that normally take on the color of the surrounding text, though some apps may show ♥ as an emoji.

Why is my heart emoji black or white?

It may be a different heart on purpose: 🖤 is a black heart emoji and 🤍 is a white one. If a red heart looks plain, it may be the text version ❤ without the emoji selector, and the app or font is drawing it as a flat glyph.

Can I use heart symbols in usernames?

Sometimes, but it depends on the app or website, because many username fields allow only certain characters. Try the heart in the field, and keep your username readable without it in case it is rejected or removed.

Are copied hearts the same on every phone?

No. The character is the same, but each device draws it using its own fonts and emoji artwork, so the shape and color can differ. Newer emoji may also show as boxes on older software, so classic hearts like ♥ and ♡ are usually the safer choice.
