Snapchat Fonts: How to Get Fancy Text for Captions, Names and Chats

Updated November 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Snapchat does not let you install your own font for the whole app, but you can still get stylish text in two ways: the caption styling the app offers in its own editor, and styled Unicode characters that you paste or type in. This guide explains the difference, where each approach tends to work, and how to avoid unreadable or hard-to-find text.

Can you change the font on Snapchat?

Not in the sense of switching the app's interface to another typeface. What you can do depends on which of two things you mean by "font".

Built-in caption styling vs styled Unicode text

Snapchat's camera and editing tools have included text options for captions on Snaps and Stories. Those tools, and the styles they offer, change over time, so open the app and look at what your version currently provides rather than relying on a screenshot or an old tutorial. Built-in styling is applied by the app itself, so it only exists inside that editor.

If you want to try the built-in options, a general approach is to take or open a Snap, look through the editing tools for a text or caption option, type a few words, and then check whether your version shows any style choices. Icon names, positions and available styles vary between versions, so treat this as a starting point and not a fixed set of steps.

Styled Unicode text is different. The letters themselves are different characters, so you can use them anywhere that accepts them, and they travel with the message. The trade-off is that you rely on each device and app to display them properly.

Why "fonts" here usually means special characters, not installed fonts

When a generator or keyboard turns hello into a bold or script look, it swaps ordinary letters for look-alike characters from the Unicode standard, such as mathematical bold letters, circled letters or fullwidth letters. Nothing is installed on your phone and the system font does not change. That is why the same text can look slightly different on another device, and why it occasionally shows as empty boxes.

Where styled text can work on Snapchat

Which fields accept special characters can change with app updates, so test before you rely on anything. A quick check is to paste a short styled phrase and see whether it stays intact after you save or send it.

  • Snap and Story captions: Usually the easiest place to try styled text, since a caption is just text you enter over the image.
  • Chat messages: Short styled phrases generally work in messages, but the recipient's device decides how they look.
  • Display name and bio: These fields can have their own rules and limits. Check what the app accepts now, and read the next section before changing your name.
  • Spotlight or public descriptions: Public text may be seen on many different devices, which raises the chance of boxes or odd spacing. Keep it simple.

Display names: be careful with styled text

Searchability: friends may not find a styled name

Search usually matches ordinary letters. A name written in styled characters is a different set of characters from the plain version, so a friend typing your name may not find you. If people need to find you easily, keep the searchable part plain.

Do not copy another person's or brand's name or style

Styled characters can make a name look almost identical to someone else's. Do not use them to imitate a person, a creator or a brand. It is misleading, and it can break the platform's rules.

Keep a plain-text version of your username in mind

When you share your account on a poster, in a bio elsewhere or out loud, give the plain version. A decorative display name is fine for personality, but the plain username is what people will actually type.

A full conversation in decorative letters is tiring to read. Use a styled word for emphasis and keep the rest plain.

Why styled text shows up as boxes or looks wrong

Unicode support differs between devices

Different phones, operating system versions and built-in fonts support different Unicode characters. If a device lacks a glyph for a character, it may show an empty box or a question mark, or it may fall back to a different-looking symbol. Rarer styles, such as some script or blackletter-like sets, often seem more likely to fail than bold or bubble letters, though this varies from device to device and app to app.

What to do about it

  • Pick a simpler style, such as bold or small caps.
  • Send the text to yourself or to a friend first and check the result.
  • Add a plain-text fallback when the information matters, like a time or a place.

Keep styled text readable and accessible

Screen readers may read styled characters letter by letter, describe them with their Unicode names (for example, "mathematical bold small g"), or skip them. That can make a message very hard to follow for someone who relies on one.

  • Use styled text for short phrases, not whole messages.
  • Combine styled words with plain text for anything important, such as dates, directions or names.
  • Avoid stacking many symbols between words, which adds more noise for assistive tools.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change the Snapchat font on Android?

You cannot change the font of the whole app. You can use whatever caption text styling the app currently offers in its editor, or type styled Unicode characters into text fields that accept them. Options change between versions, so check your current app.

Can I use fancy text in my display name?

It may be possible, depending on what the app currently accepts, but be cautious. Friends searching in plain letters may not find a name written in styled characters, so keep your plain username handy to share. Never use styled text to copy someone else's name.

Why do my styled letters look like boxes?

Styled text uses Unicode characters, and not every device or system font includes all of them. When a character is missing, you may see a box or a placeholder. Try a simpler style like bold, or send a test message first.

Is fancy text accessible?

Not always. Screen readers may spell out styled characters in an awkward way or skip them. Use styled text for short phrases and put important details in plain text.
