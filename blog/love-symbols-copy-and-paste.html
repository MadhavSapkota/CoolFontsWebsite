Love Symbols to Copy and Paste (Plus What They Mean)

Updated November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Love symbols are characters, shortcuts and small pictures people use to express affection: hearts like ♡ and ♥, the infinity sign ∞, <3, kisses, and love kaomoji. Copy any symbol below, then paste it into a bio, caption or message. Further down you will find what traditional symbols of love mean, how text symbols differ from emoji, and why some show up as empty boxes.

Love symbols to copy and paste

Most of these are Unicode characters, so they behave like text. They are not images, which means they can take on the font and colour of the text around them. They also mean that each device decides how to draw them.

Heart characters

♡ is an outline heart, ♥ is a solid heart, ❣ is a heart with an exclamation-style dot, and ❥ is a rotated, decorative heart. We cover these in more detail, including text hearts versus emoji hearts, in our heart symbol copy and paste guide.

Beyond hearts

Love is not only hearts. These characters are often used for romance, devotion or a love-note feel:

  • ∞ is the infinity sign, often used for "forever" or "endless".
  • ✉ is an envelope, handy for love letters or notes.
  • ➳ is a feathered arrow, sometimes used as a nod to Cupid's arrow.
  • ⚭ is a marriage symbol made of linked rings. It is a less common character, so test it before relying on it.
  • ✿ and ✧ are decorative marks (a flower and a sparkle). They suit a romantic, soft look, but they are not love symbols by definition.

Text shortcuts like <3 and xoxo

<3 is a sideways heart made from two ordinary keyboard characters, so it can be typed almost anywhere. Some apps and keyboards automatically swap it for a heart emoji, so what you type may not be what the other person sees. "xoxo" is a common sign-off for hugs and kisses (x for kiss, o for hug).

Love kaomoji and text faces

Kaomoji are faces built from characters. They carry more feeling than a lone heart, which suits a love note or a cute reply.

Love symbol vs love emoji: what is the difference?

A love symbol in text and a love emoji can look similar but are handled differently by your phone.

Text symbol (♡ ♥ ∞)Emoji (❤️ 💌 💍)
What it isA Unicode character drawn from a fontA character drawn by your device's emoji set
ColourUsually follows the surrounding text colourUsually fixed, full colour
Look across devicesDepends on the font each device falls back toDiffers by platform, because each draws its own version
Best forSubtle, minimal or decorative touchesClear, expressive, instantly recognisable feeling

Some characters, such as ♥ and ❣, exist in both a text style and an emoji style. Which one you see depends on the app and device, so the same pasted character can look plain in one place and colourful in another.

When to choose which: use emoji when you want the feeling to be obvious and warm. Use text symbols when you want something small, tidy and colour-matched, such as in a bio line or a display name.

What do traditional love symbols mean?

These are general cultural associations, not fixed rules. Meanings shift between cultures, eras and individual relationships.

The heart

The heart shape is the best-known symbol of love today. It is not a realistic picture of the organ, and where the shape came from is debated, so it is safer to say it has long been used as a sign of affection, especially in Western visual culture.

Roses, doves and Cupid

  • Roses: red roses are widely associated with romantic love in many Western traditions.
  • Doves: often linked with peace, and a pair of doves is commonly used in wedding imagery to suggest devotion.
  • Cupid's arrow: Cupid is the Roman god of desire, and the arrow is shorthand for someone suddenly falling in love.

Infinity signs and rings

The infinity sign suggests something without end, so people use it for "forever" messages. Rings are circles with no start or finish, which is one reason they are popular symbols of lasting commitment.

If you are unsure how a symbol will land, especially with someone from a different background, pair it with plain words.

Which love symbol should you use where?

  • Bios and display names: one or two small symbols, such as ♡ or ∞, work better than a long chain. Keep your actual name readable.
  • Captions and comments: put a heart or kaomoji at the end of the sentence, after the words that carry the meaning.
  • Messages, anniversary notes and cards: write the message in plain words first, then add one symbol, such as "Happy anniversary ♡". Emoji or a kaomoji add warmth in casual chats.
  • Usernames and handles: many platforms limit allowed characters, and symbols can make a name hard to search or type. Keep a plain-text version of your name that people can find easily.

Decorative characters that look like love symbols but are not

ღ is often pasted as a love mark because it looks a bit like a heart-shaped loop. It is actually a letter from the Georgian alphabet, and other borrowed decorative letters have their own original meanings too. That does not make them wrong to use, but it is worth knowing.

A screen reader may announce a letter like ღ as the letter it is, or as a character name, rather than as "love". In a bio or caption that can interrupt the reading flow, so use such marks sparingly.

Why a love symbol shows up as a box or looks wrong

Missing glyphs

If you see an empty square or a question-mark box, the font on that device or in that app has no picture for that character. Rarer characters, such as ⚭, are more likely to fail than common ones like ♥ or ∞.

Text style vs emoji style

As noted above, a character such as ♥ may appear as a flat symbol in one app and as a coloured emoji in another. Neither is a mistake. It is how that app chooses to display it.

Picking safer characters

  1. Prefer common characters: ♥, ♡, ∞, and plain <3.
  2. Paste your text into a draft, a note to yourself or a private chat first to check it.
  3. If a symbol breaks, replace it with a more common one or with an emoji.

Display can differ from phone to phone and app to app, so a quick test beats assuming.

Using love symbols accessibly

Screen readers may read a symbol's full Unicode name, say something short, or skip it entirely, depending on the software. A string of ten hearts can be tiring to hear. A few habits help:

  • Put symbols at the end of a line rather than in the middle of a sentence.
  • Avoid long runs of the same character.
  • Do not replace words with symbols. Write "I love you", then add ♡ if you like.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common love symbol?

The heart is the most widely used one, in text forms like ♡ and ♥, in emoji like ❤️, and as the keyboard shortcut <3. After that, the infinity sign ∞ and the kiss shorthand xoxo are common choices for romantic messages.

Can I use love symbols in a username?

Sometimes, but it depends on the platform, since many restrict which characters are allowed. Symbols can also make a name harder to search or type, so it helps to keep a plain-text version of your name and use a symbol only as a small extra.

Why does <3 sometimes turn into a heart emoji?

Some keyboards and apps automatically replace the text shortcut with a heart emoji as you type or send. If you want to keep the sideways heart exactly as typed, check how the app handles it, or use a text heart like ♡ instead.

Do love symbols look the same on every phone?

No. Text symbols are drawn from whatever font the device or app uses, and emoji are drawn by each platform's own emoji set. Common characters like ♥ and ∞ are usually safe, but it is worth testing before you post anything important.
