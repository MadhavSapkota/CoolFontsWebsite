Facebook Font Changer: How to Get Bold, Cursive and Fancy Text

Updated November 15, 2026 · 8 min read

A Facebook font changer cannot change the font of the Facebook app. It converts your words into Unicode lookalike characters (bold, cursive, bubble and so on) that you can paste or type into a post, comment or bio. Use the generator at the top of this page to preview styles, then read on to learn where styled text works, where to be careful, and how to keep it readable.

Can you change the font on Facebook?

What Facebook's own text options do and do not include

Facebook does not offer a font picker where you choose a typeface for your profile or the whole app. Its composer options change over time, so open the post box in your own app to see what is currently offered. Any formatting you find there is separate from the tools in this article.

What a Facebook font changer really does: Unicode characters, not installed fonts

The stylish text from font changers is not a font in the usual sense. Unicode includes many extra letter shapes, such as mathematical bold letters, script letters and circled letters. A font changer swaps each plain letter for one of these look-alikes. Because they are ordinary text characters, you can paste them into many places that accept text. The same trait causes the problems covered later: a device or app that lacks a glyph may show an empty box instead.

For a deeper explanation of how this works, see our fancy text generator guide.

What it cannot do: change the app font or your phone's system font

A font changer will not restyle menus, buttons, other people's posts or your friends' comments. It will not change your phone's system font either. Only the text you type or paste is affected, and people see it in whatever their own device uses to draw those characters.

Fancy text styles to use on Facebook

Copy any example below and paste it into a Facebook field to test it.

Bold and italic-style text

Bold-style letters are a common choice for short headlines, because they stand out without looking too decorative. Italic-style letters are softer and suit quotes or captions.

Cursive and script

Script styles look handwritten and suit greetings, wedding or family posts, and short signatures. They are harder to read in long passages, so keep them to a few words. For more options, see cursive fonts copy and paste.

Bubble and cute styles

Circled letters look playful and work for party invitations, birthday wishes and light-hearted comments. They are quite wide, so a word or two goes a long way.

Gothic / Old English style (use sparingly)

Blackletter-style text has a dramatic, formal look. It is difficult to read at small sizes, so save it for a single word, such as a heading for a themed event or Group.

Small decorations: hearts, stars and kaomoji

Sometimes a symbol or a kaomoji adds more personality than restyled letters. Kaomoji are faces built from text characters, and they suit comments and Messenger chats.

Where styled text can work on Facebook

Posts and comments

Posts and comments generally accept pasted text, so this is where most people try styled text first. A short bold headline above a plain-text message is a good pattern.

Intro and bio

Short fields like an intro line suit a little decoration, since only a few words are involved. If a character is not accepted or displays oddly, remove it and use plain text.

Messenger chats

Chat is a casual place for kaomoji, hearts and bubble-style words. Whether the other person sees them correctly depends on their device.

Groups and Page descriptions

Group rules, welcome posts and Page descriptions are read by many people on many devices, so they carry more risk. Style a heading if you like, and keep the actual rules, hours, links and contact details in plain text.

Why you should test in each place before relying on it

We cannot promise that every Facebook field handles every Unicode character the same way. Facebook can also change how it displays text. Paste your text into the specific field you plan to use, check how it looks, and ideally ask a friend on a different phone to confirm.

Names on Facebook: be careful with styled text

Why a name field is different from a post

A post is just content. A name is how people find you, tag you and recognise you, and it may be reviewed by Facebook. A name in decorative characters may be hard to search for and could be flagged or rejected.

Check Facebook's current name rules in its Help Center

Name rules can change, and we cannot say what Facebook currently accepts for profile or Page names. Read the naming guidance in Facebook's own Help Center before editing a name, and treat plain text as the safe choice.

Never use styled text to impersonate someone or get around name checks

Styled characters should never be used to copy another person's or business's name, or to slip past a name review. That misleads people and can get an account into trouble. Keep styling for your own posts and personal flourishes.

How to pick a style that suits your post

Announcements and headlines: short styled lines

For an event, a milestone or a celebration, style only the first line or the key phrase. The reader sees what matters straight away and the rest stays easy to read.

Personal posts: a light touch

For a birthday comment or a family update, a few styled words and a heart or kaomoji are plenty. Our heart symbol guide explains the difference between text hearts and emoji hearts.

Business and Page posts: keep the key info in plain text

Prices, dates, addresses, links and phone numbers should stay in ordinary characters. People search for those details, copy them and rely on them. A styled heading is fine, but the information underneath should be plain.

Why styled text shows as boxes or looks wrong

Missing glyphs on some devices and apps

Every Unicode style depends on the viewer's device having a glyph for each character. If it does not, it may show an empty box or a question mark. Some styles are supported more widely than others, and less common ones are more likely to break, so test before you rely on one.

Search, hashtags and links work better in plain text

Search treats a styled word as different characters from the plain word, so people searching for your keyword may not find it. Hashtags and links should stay in plain text so they remain clickable and searchable.

Quick test: send it to yourself or a friend on another device

  1. Paste the styled text into a Messenger chat with a friend who is happy to help, or send it to another device you use.
  2. Open it on a second device if you have one, or ask the friend what they see.
  3. If you see boxes or odd gaps, switch to a more common style or go back to plain text.

Keep Facebook text readable and accessible

Screen readers can read styled characters strangely or skip them

Styled letters are not letters to a screen reader. They are special symbols with names like mathematical bold small h. Depending on the software, they may be read out letter by letter in a confusing way, or skipped entirely. We have not tested specific screen readers, so treat this as a reason for caution, not a precise claim about any one tool.

Keep important details, links and hashtags in plain text

If someone needs to know a time, place, price or instruction, put it in ordinary text. Use styled text for decoration only.

Avoid styling long paragraphs

A whole paragraph in script or blackletter is tiring to read for everyone. Limit styling to a short headline, a few words or a decorative line.

Copy-and-paste generators vs a keyboard

How the generator-site workflow goes: type, copy, switch apps, paste

With a copy-and-paste generator, you type your text, choose a style, copy the result, switch to Facebook and paste it. That works well for a one-off headline or when you want to compare many styles side by side.

How typing with a keyboard avoids switching apps

A styled-text keyboard puts the styles inside the keyboard itself, so you type straight into the Facebook field without leaving it. That matters most for comments and chats, where repeatedly switching apps gets tedious.

Where each approach makes sense

  • Generator: good for planning one post, previewing styles and copying a single line.
  • Keyboard: good if you style text often, reply in chats or want kaomoji and symbols close at hand.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Facebook font changer change my whole app?

No. A font changer only produces text in special Unicode characters that you paste or type. The menus, buttons and other people's posts keep their normal look, and your phone's system font stays the same.

Will my friends see the same styled text?

Often, but not always. Each person's device must have a glyph for every character, and if it does not, they may see empty boxes. Send a test to a friend on a different phone before using a style in something important.

Can I use fancy text in my Facebook name?

We can't say what Facebook currently accepts, and styled characters in names may be restricted. Check the naming rules in Facebook's Help Center first, and use plain text if in doubt. Never use styled characters to copy someone else's name or avoid a name check.

Is fancy text bad for accessibility?

It can be. Screen readers may read styled characters strangely or skip them, so the message can be lost. Use styled text for short decorative lines only, and keep details, links and hashtags in plain text.
