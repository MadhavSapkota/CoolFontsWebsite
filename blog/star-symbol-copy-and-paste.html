Star Symbol Copy and Paste: Text Stars, Sparkles and How to Use Them

Updated November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

To copy and paste a star symbol, tap one in the copy picker on this page or in the example buttons below, then paste it into your bio, caption, note or chat. ★ is a solid star, ☆ is an outline, and ✦ ✧ are four-point sparkle stars. Most of them are plain Unicode text characters, so they take on the color and font of the text around them, but a few can turn into emoji or show up as an empty box on some devices.

The rest of this guide explains how the star characters differ, which one suits which job, and what to do when one does not display properly.

Star symbols to copy and paste

Star characters come from several parts of Unicode, which is why they look so different from one another. Here are the main families, with what to expect from each.

Classic stars: ★ ☆

These are the safe default. ★ (black star) and ☆ (white star) suit ratings, bullets and light decoration, and they sit naturally beside ordinary text.

Sparkle and four-point stars: ✦ ✧ ✨

✦ and ✧ give the thin, pointed sparkle look that is common in aesthetic bios. ✨ looks similar in spirit, but it is an emoji, so it renders as a small colored picture rather than a text glyph.

Decorative and outlined stars

Characters like ✩ ✯ ✰ add variety when ★ feels too plain. Because they are rarer, test them where they will be seen before you build a whole design around them.

Star symbol vs star emoji: what is the difference?

The short version: ★ and ☆ are characters, while ⭐ and 🌟 are emoji. They can look alike at a glance, but they behave differently.

  • Text characters such as ★ ☆ ✦ ✧ inherit the font, size and color of the text next to them. Put one in a gray caption and it is gray.
  • Emoji such as ⭐ ✨ 🌟 💫 are drawn by the device or app, usually in fixed colors, and the artwork differs between platforms. The same ⭐ will not look identical everywhere.
  • Some characters sit in between. ✨ and ⭐ are treated as emoji by default, while ★ and ☆ usually behave as text. A few others, like ✳ and ✴, have both text and emoji forms, so an app may show either one.

If you want a star that matches your text style, choose a text character. If you want a bright, colorful pop, choose an emoji. The same logic applies to hearts, which our heart symbol guide covers in more detail.

Which star should you use where?

Bios and display names

Platforms set their own rules for names and usernames. Some usernames allow only letters, numbers and a few punctuation marks, while display names are often more flexible. Check what the field accepts, because a symbol that works in a bio may be rejected or stripped in a username.

Captions, posts and comments

Stars work well as sparkle accents around a short phrase. They are also a quiet way to separate lines without using a heading.

Dividers and bullet points

A star at the start of a line makes a tidy bullet, and a short row works as a divider.

Star ratings with ★ and ☆

Fill the stars with ★ and leave the rest as ☆. Pair the stars with the number so the meaning does not depend on the symbols alone.

Documents, notes and spreadsheets

In a document or sheet, stick with ★ and ☆. They are the most likely to display the same way for the people who open the file. Avoid the rarer decorative stars in anything you will share widely or print.

Why a star shows up as a box or looks wrong

Unicode characters and font support

Each star is a Unicode character, and Unicode only defines the character. The font on the device draws the actual shape. If the current font has no glyph for it, you may see an empty box, a question mark or a different fallback symbol.

Older devices and apps with limited fonts

Older systems and apps with restricted font choices are more likely to lack the rarer characters like ✫ ✬ ✭ ✮ or some circled variants. Newer devices usually have more coverage, but coverage still varies from device to device.

Stars that change into emoji or change size

Some star-like characters have both a plain text form and an emoji form. Depending on the app, the same character can appear as a thin black shape in one place and a colorful picture in another. Stars from different Unicode blocks can also look larger or smaller next to your text, since they may come from different fonts.

How to pick safer, more widely supported stars

  • Start with ★ and ☆. These tend to be among the most widely supported.
  • Use ✦ and ✧ for a sparkle look. They are common, but test them first.
  • Use the rarer decorative stars sparingly and not in anything essential.

Check your result on the platform where it will be seen

Paste a test version into the app, post or document, and look at it. If you can, check on another device too, since the person reading it may have different fonts than you. If a character breaks, swap it for ★ or ☆.

Using stars accessibly

How screen readers may announce star characters

Screen readers may read a symbol by its Unicode name, for example "black star", or skip it entirely. This varies by software, so do not count on one behavior.

Avoid long runs of stars

A row like ★★★★★★★★★★ may be announced as ten separate stars, which gets tiring to listen to. Keep decoration to one to three stars, and place them at the edges of a line rather than between every word.

Add a text equivalent for ratings

If you show a rating with ★ and ☆, write it out too: "★★★★☆ 4 out of 5". Then the rating is clear whether or not the symbols are read aloud or display correctly.

Do not rely on a star alone to carry meaning

If the star matters, such as marking a favorite or a rating, say so in words. Treat the symbol as a visual extra.

Frequently asked questions

Is a star symbol the same as a star emoji?

No. Characters like ★ and ☆ are text, so they follow the font and color of the surrounding words. Emoji such as ⭐ and 🌟 are drawn as small colored pictures by the device, so their design varies between platforms.

Why is my star a box?

The font being used does not include a glyph for that character, so the app shows a placeholder box or question mark. This is more likely with rarer decorative stars and on older devices. Switching to ★ or ☆ usually solves it.

Can I use star symbols in a username?

It depends on the platform. Some username fields accept only letters, numbers and a few punctuation marks, while display names are often more flexible. Try the star in the field itself and see whether it is accepted or removed.

Do star symbols work everywhere?

Not always. Support depends on the fonts of the device and app showing them. ★ and ☆ tend to be the most widely supported, while rarer decorative stars are more likely to appear as boxes. Test the result where it will be viewed.

What is the difference between ★ and ☆?

★ is the black star, which is solid, and ☆ is the white star, which is outlined. They are often used together, for example in star ratings, where ★ marks the filled stars and ☆ the empty ones.
